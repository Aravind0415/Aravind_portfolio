Entry 01 — McKesson · Feb 2025–Present

Validating AI-assisted anomaly detection against accounting logic

✓Human
Verified
+20%Forecast accuracy

The Challenge

McKesson piloted an AI-assisted anomaly detection system to flag irregularities in large-scale B2B financial data. The system could surface statistical outliers, but had no way to judge whether a flagged item was a genuine error, a legitimate one-off, or a data artifact. That judgment call needed a person with real accounting expertise sitting between the model's output and the executive dashboard.

The Approach

  • Reviewed flagged anomalies individually against underlying accounting entries, applying GAAP-based reasoning and historical variance benchmarks rather than accepting the model's flag at face value.
  • Built a repeatable evaluation process: every flagged item checked against source documentation, the relevant regulatory filing, and prior-period trends before classification as true positive, false positive, or context-dependent.
  • Fed classification decisions back into the workflow — acting as the human-in-the-loop layer separating statistical noise from real financial risk.

The Result

This review discipline became the basis for a broader modeling effort — designing Azure/SQL-integrated forecasting models cross-validated against historical variance benchmarks — which lifted forecast accuracy by 20%. Executive reporting turnaround also shortened, since automated Power BI dashboards still passed through the same validation before publication.

Why it matters for AI evaluation: This is model evaluation work under a different job title — an AI system produces an output, a domain expert checks it against ground truth, and that judgment decides whether it's trustworthy enough to act on. The same discipline transfers directly to evaluating AI-generated financial statements or finance-domain model responses.

Entry 02 — McKesson · Feb 2025–Present

Designing a forecast model judged against its own track record

Financial Analyst
✓Human
Verified

The Challenge

Prior forecasting approaches weren't systematically cross-validated against historical variance, making it hard to know when a forecast should be trusted versus flagged for review.

The Approach

  • Designed forecasting models integrated with Azure and SQL, structuring large, messy B2B datasets into a form that could be validated systematically.
  • Cross-validated outputs against historical variance benchmarks — the forecasting equivalent of checking an AI model's output against a labeled evaluation set.
  • Embedded internal control checkpoints directly into segment-level reporting, catching discrepancies before they reached executive dashboards rather than after.

The Result

Forecast accuracy improved by 20%, and the control checkpoints reduced the number of unflagged discrepancies reaching leadership. The validation logic itself became reusable — later applied to reviewing AI-flagged anomalies (Entry 01).

Why it matters for AI evaluation: Building a forecast model and building an evaluation harness require the same underlying skill — defining what "correct" looks like from historical ground truth, then measuring new outputs against that baseline consistently and catching drift early.

The Challenge

Mastercard's recurring reporting cycles were slow, and financial statements spanning multiple periods needed reconciling accurately without a supervisor checking every step — a setting that required trustworthy independent judgment, not just technical execution.

The Approach

  • Automated recurring reporting workflows in Power BI and SQL, cutting cycle time nearly in half and freeing time for higher-precision manual review of market reports and statements.
  • Reconciled multi-cycle financial statements independently, validating accuracy on distributed, cross-functional projects with no direct oversight.
  • Translated complex fiscal data into decision-ready dashboards for non-financial stakeholders, flagging discrepancies before executive review.

The Result

Audit readiness improved as a direct result of the reconciliation discipline, and expense analysis across business units surfaced cost-saving opportunities ahead of quarterly budget cycles.

Why it matters for AI evaluation: Evaluation work is frequently unsupervised in the same way — handed an output and a rubric, an evaluator has to make a defensible correctness judgment alone. This is a track record of doing exactly that, at speed, without oversight.

The Challenge

A growing B2B software firm had no budgeting and forecasting framework, and no way to verify accuracy across B2B contracts and regulatory submissions — all under tight, ad hoc turnarounds.

The Approach

  • Built budgeting and forecasting frameworks from the ground up, critically reviewing regulatory filings, market reports, and accounting data for logical consistency before they went live.
  • Verified accuracy across B2B contracts and regulatory submissions through independent document evaluation on tight-deadline, ad hoc assignments.
  • Automated recurring Power BI reporting to cut manual data-entry errors, reinforcing numerical literacy through repeated cross-checks against source data.

The Result

Planning accuracy improved as the framework matured, manual errors dropped after automation, and the firm gained a repeatable process for evaluating contracts and submissions rather than relying on one-off review.

Why it matters for AI evaluation: This shows the ability to design an evaluation framework where none existed — defining the criteria, building the review process, iterating on it — rather than only operating inside a framework someone else built. That transfers directly to standing up new AI evaluation rubrics.
